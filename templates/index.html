📚 What is Unix Time?

Unix time (also known as Epoch time or POSIX time) is a system for describing points in time. It's defined as the number of seconds that have elapsed since 00:00:00 UTC on January 1, 1970, not counting leap seconds.

This moment is called the "Unix Epoch" and serves as the reference point for all Unix timestamps.

🎂
The Birth of Unix
Unix time started on January 1, 1970, because that's when the Unix operating system was being developed at Bell Labs.
⚠️
Y2K38 Problem
On January 19, 2038, 32-bit systems will overflow at 2,147,483,647 seconds, causing the "Year 2038 problem."
🌍
Universal Standard
Unix time is timezone-independent and always represents UTC, making it perfect for global systems.
🚫
No Leap Seconds
Unix time doesn't account for leap seconds, which are occasionally added to keep atomic time in sync with Earth's rotation.
💾
Efficient Storage
Storing time as a single integer is much more efficient than storing year, month, day, hour, minute, and second separately.
🎉
Billion Second Party
Unix time reached 1 billion seconds on September 9, 2001, at 01:46:40 UTC. The next milestone is 2 billion in 2033!

⚙️ Technical Details

32-bit Systems
Can represent dates from 1970 to 2038 (signed) or 1970 to 2106 (unsigned)
64-bit Systems
Can represent dates for approximately 292 billion years into the future!
Negative Values
Negative Unix timestamps represent dates before January 1, 1970
Milliseconds
JavaScript uses milliseconds since epoch (multiply Unix time by 1000)
